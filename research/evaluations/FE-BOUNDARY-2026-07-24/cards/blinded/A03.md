# A03

- **Input:** A procedural knowledge artifact.
- **Operation:** Encode its entry, actions, decisions, branching, iteration, evidence updates, and termination using a finite typed vocabulary and explicit composition rules.
- **Output:** A normalized, executable-or-inspectable procedure graph plus unresolved elements.
- **Scope conditions:** Artifacts that prescribe reasoning or decision behavior.
- **Failure conditions:** Vocabulary cannot represent a material operation; encoding adds unstated steps; independent encoders diverge.
- **Predicted effect:** Relative to free-form summaries, normalized graphs will increase cross-representation structural agreement and defect detection without losing task-critical steps.
- **Measurement:** Graph agreement, seeded-defect recall, omitted-step rate, and execution success.
- **Implementation test:** Independent encoders process prose and diagram variants; validators check type/composition constraints.
- **Decision rights:** Encoder maps; validator rejects invalid forms; adjudicator preserves ambiguity.
- **Uncertainty handling:** Unknown and alternative subgraphs remain explicit.
- **Verification:** Round-trip reconstruction and task simulation.
- **Provenance:** Nodes link to source spans.
- **Implementation cost:** Vocabulary training and encoding.
- **Limitations:** Existing evidence addresses expressiveness/repeatability, not incremental utility. Direct: layered encoding. Inference: comparative prediction.
