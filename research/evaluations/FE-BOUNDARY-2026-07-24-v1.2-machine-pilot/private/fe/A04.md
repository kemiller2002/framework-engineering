# A04 — Separate reasoning grammar from execution/coordination grammar

- **Input [inference]:** A procedural artifact containing both reasoning functions and timing, allocation, synchronization, or sequencing functions, consistent with the two paired definitions in the theory doc's Core Definitions.
- **Operation [proposal-only]:** The theory doc defines "Procedural Reasoning Grammar" and "Execution or Coordination Grammar" as two separate hypothesized compositions, but does not describe an operation for producing or applying the split; it is a definitional distinction, not a demonstrated procedure.
- **Output [absent]:** No output artifact or representation is described in the permitted canonical set.
- **Scope conditions [direct]:** Motivated where coordination-heavy problems place pressure on the current vocabulary; Hypothesis 3 states "execution-heavy artifacts require additional coordination primitives not yet established in the accepted vocabulary."
- **Failure conditions [direct]:** The falsification criteria state the theory weakens if "reasoning and execution grammars cannot be separated."
- **Predicted effect [direct]:** "Execution-heavy artifacts will continue to pressure the current vocabulary for additional primitives such as synchronization, scheduling, allocation, sequencing, or coordination" (Predictions).
- **Measurement [absent]:** No metric is defined in the permitted canonical set.
- **Implementation test [absent]:** No test procedure is described.
- **Decision rights [absent]:** Not addressed.
- **Uncertainty handling [direct]:** The theory doc states current theory "does not yet show that reasoning grammar and execution grammar are cleanly separable," and lists as an Open Question "Are `Synchronize`, `Schedule`, `Sequence`, `Allocate`, and `Coordinate` genuine execution primitives or just domain-specific variants?" — the split is explicitly held as unresolved rather than assumed.
- **Verification [direct]:** Next Required Work calls to "Test whether reasoning and execution grammars can be separated without distorting real artifacts."
- **Provenance [absent]:** Not addressed.
- **Implementation cost [absent]:** Not addressed.
- **Limitations [direct]:** The theory doc's fourth provisional claim only states primitive sufficiency "appears weaker" in coordination-heavy problems, not that a second grammar has been established; the Open Questions section treats the separability of the two grammars itself as unresolved: "Do reasoning and execution grammars separate cleanly, or are they entangled in practice?"

- **Direct:** Scope motivation, failure/falsification condition, predicted effect, explicit unresolved status, and verification requirement.
- **Inference:** Input framing drawn from the paired definitions.
- **Proposal-only:** The two-grammar distinction itself, which exists in the theory document only as a definitional pairing and an open hypothesis, not as a demonstrated operation.
