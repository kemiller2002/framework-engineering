# RFR-2026-012: Cognitive-load and usability study

## Research opportunity

Test whether explicit evidence, uncertainty, and provenance controls improve decisions without unacceptable cognitive load.

## Background

The architecture assumes human/machine use, but usability and cognitive burden are not validated.

**Specific assumption challenged:** More explicit structure improves net usefulness for humans.

**Supporting evidence:** Traceability improvement is a claim under test; the boundary REP states net benefit is untested.

## Unknowns

- Comprehension burden
- Expertise interaction
- Error-detection versus completion-time tradeoff

## Dependencies

- [RFR-2026-003](./RFR-2026-003.md)

## Suggested REP and methodology

Create `REP-2026-012`. Run task-based usability studies with novice/expert strata; measure comprehension, time, errors, confidence calibration, and abandonment; compare progressively disclosed variants.

## Expected outputs

- Usability protocol
- Interaction logs
- Cognitive-load report

## Success criteria

Benefits and harms are reported by expertise level with predefined minimally important differences.
