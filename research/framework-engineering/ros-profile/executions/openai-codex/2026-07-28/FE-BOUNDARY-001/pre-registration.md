## Research question

Does Framework Engineering have a useful non-redundant boundary relative to
adjacent disciplines, or is it better classified as an integrated method
profile or a repository-specific research program?

## Frozen candidate explanations

1. **Distinct discipline** — FE has a coherent object of practice and at least
   one non-subsumed mechanism or outcome whose independent utility can be
   tested.
2. **Integrated method profile** — FE composes established mechanisms around
   frameworks as the unit of analysis; the integration may be useful without
   being a new discipline.
3. **Repository-specific research program** — FE is currently an internally
   coherent local program whose transfer and generality are unproven.
4. **Full subsumption** — adjacent disciplines collectively or individually
   cover FE without meaningful loss; discipline-building should stop.

## Frozen comparison dimensions

1. Primary object and unit of analysis.
2. Intended users, decisions, and contexts.
3. Lifecycle coverage: discovery, design, validation, operation, evolution,
   retirement.
4. Core operations and claimed causal mechanisms.
5. Representation, semantics, and implementation independence.
6. Evidence, measurement, verification, validation, and falsification.
7. Configuration, versioning, provenance, traceability, and change control.
8. Context adaptation, tailoring, and boundary conditions.
9. Human roles, organizational adoption, governance, and ethics.
10. Automation, tooling, interoperability, and machine/agent use.
11. Outcomes, failure modes, burden, and cost-effectiveness.
12. Institutional maturity: body of knowledge, education, standards,
    community, credentialing, and independent practice.

## Inclusion and evidence rules

- Prefer standards bodies, official handbooks, original peer-reviewed
  research, and direct technical records.
- Use secondary sources only for orientation or explicit critique.
- Record inaccessible standards as abstract-only rather than inferring their
  contents.
- Separate direct source claims, repository observations, synthesis
  inferences, and missing evidence.
- Do not count terminology, integration, documentation volume, or the
  existence of tools as evidence of distinctiveness.
- Frequency of a mechanism is descriptive, not proof of value.

## Decision rules

- **Distinct** requires a coherent boundary plus at least one plausible
  non-subsumed mechanism or outcome and a feasible discriminating experiment.
- **Integrated profile** is preferred when FE's object-level integration is
  coherent but mechanisms are inherited.
- **Repository program** is preferred when coherence exists but transfer,
  independent use, and comparative utility are missing.
- **Full subsumption** is supported when an adjacent field covers the same
  object, lifecycle, mechanisms, and outcomes without meaningful loss.
- Literature can establish boundary overlap and research hypotheses; it
  cannot establish FE's causal utility.
